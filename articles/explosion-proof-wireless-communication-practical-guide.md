# Explosion-Proof Wireless Communication: A Practical Guide for Hazardous Areas

Explosion-proof wireless communication combines two separate engineering disciplines: hazardous-area protection and radio/network design. A project is acceptable only when the exact equipment and installed configuration suit the classified location and the network meets coverage, capacity, availability, security, and maintenance requirements.

## Start with the Safety Boundary

Before selecting an AP, bridge, router, DTU, client, or wireless board, document:

- destination country and accepted certification scheme;
- Zone or Division, gas or dust group, temperature class or maximum surface temperature;
- required Equipment Protection Level;
- ambient range and environmental exposure;
- cable entries, antennas, power, mounting, earthing, and accessories;
- exact certificate, schedule, special conditions, and installation instructions.

Terms such as industrial, rugged, stainless steel, or an ingress-protection rating do not by themselves establish hazardous-area suitability. Do not treat intrinsic safety, flameproof protection, and explosion-proof as interchangeable design claims.

## Choose the Network Function

- Use an access point for local WiFi coverage to tablets, terminals, cameras, and mobile equipment.
- Use a wireless bridge to extend Ethernet across a defined line-of-sight path.
- Use a cellular router when routed IP, VPN, firewall, multi-device access, or WAN failover is required.
- Use a DTU for focused serial or telemetry transport when full IP networking is unnecessary.
- Use a board-level radio only when the OEM controls the complete host design and final approval path.

## Engineering Acceptance

1. Survey the site and create a path, coverage, and interference model.
2. Test with representative clients, antennas, mounting positions, and traffic.
3. Measure signal level, SNR, retries, channel utilization, throughput, latency, and recovery behavior.
4. Validate roaming on the actual mobile route and application session.
5. Verify backbone capacity, redundancy, power, grounding, surge protection, and maintenance access.
6. Segment OT traffic, restrict management access, protect credentials, and control firmware updates.
7. Preserve the approved configuration and apply formal change control.

No distance, PHY-rate, device-count, or enclosure claim should be approved without its stated conditions. A successful prototype in an office does not validate a refinery, mine, chemical plant, or other metal-rich hazardous environment.
