# What Does Ex tb IIIC T80°C Db Mean?

`Ex tb IIIC T80°C Db` is a hazardous-area marking for equipment protected against combustible dust by an enclosure. It must be interpreted as a complete marking together with the certificate, schedule, ambient range, special conditions, and installation instructions.

## Marking Breakdown

| Element | Engineering meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `Ex` | Equipment uses an explosion-protection concept |
| `tb` | Protection by enclosure for dust atmospheres at protection level `b` |
| `IIIC` | Conductive-dust group; the exact certificate defines covered conditions |
| `T80°C` | Maximum marked surface temperature under the certified conditions |
| `Db` | Equipment Protection Level for dust atmospheres |

The marking does not mean that the product is suitable for every dust, ambient temperature, location, or installation. The site owner must compare the required classification with the exact equipment certificate and configuration.

## Verification Checklist

- Confirm the combustible material and whether dust is conductive.
- Obtain the site Zone and required Equipment Protection Level.
- Check the ignition characteristics and required surface-temperature margin.
- Verify the marked ambient range and any special conditions of use.
- Confirm the exact model, revision, enclosure, entries, glands, antennas, fasteners, seals, and accessories.
- Review ingress protection, corrosion, impact, cleaning, dust accumulation, and maintenance requirements separately.
- Ensure installation and inspection follow the applicable regional rules and certificate instructions.

Do not equate an IP rating with explosion protection. Ingress protection can support the enclosure concept, but the hazardous-area marking and certificate establish the approved protection method. Likewise, do not remove, substitute, drill, or modify enclosure parts without controlled engineering and certification review.

## Procurement Record

For each purchased unit, retain the model, serial or batch identification, nameplate marking, certificate and schedule, datasheet revision, installation instructions, approved accessories, inspection records, and change history. Record missing documents instead of inferring compliance.
