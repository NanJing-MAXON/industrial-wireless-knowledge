# How to Select an Explosion-Proof Wireless Access Point

Select an explosion-proof wireless access point through two gates. First, verify that the exact model, marking, certificate, and installed configuration match the hazardous area. Second, design the WLAN around the actual clients, coverage, airtime, roaming, security, backhaul, power, and maintenance requirements. A high PHY rate or ingress-protection rating cannot compensate for unsuitable hazardous-area approval or poor RF design.

## Gate 1: Hazardous-Area Qualification

Record the governing Zone or Division, gas or dust group, temperature class or maximum surface temperature, equipment protection level, ambient range, and special conditions of use. Compare the exact model and revision against the current certificate and schedule. Verify antennas, cable entries, glands, power method, mounting, earthing, and permitted ambient conditions.

IP ratings describe ingress protection; they do not establish hazardous-location suitability.

## Gate 2: WLAN Engineering

Build a client inventory covering:

- device type and radio capability;
- supported bands, channel widths, MIMO, and security;
- fixed, portable, or continuously moving operation;
- telemetry, voice, video, file transfer, or control-support traffic;
- acceptable outage and roaming interruption;
- platform and driver lifecycle.

WiFi 6 can improve efficiency when compatible clients compete for airtime, but it does not automatically improve coverage, latency, or roaming. Validate the design with the real terminal, camera, AGV, or robot rather than a phone alone.

## Deployment Checks

1. Model pipe racks, vessels, ducts, walls, vehicles, and other RF obstructions.
2. Survey signal level, SNR, channel utilization, retries, and application behavior.
3. Size capacity from active-client airtime and traffic, not association count.
4. Test 802.11k/v/r roaming end to end with the actual client and application.
5. Verify Ethernet, fiber, or wireless backhaul and the approved power arrangement.
6. Segment operational, maintenance, video, and guest traffic where appropriate.
7. Restrict the management plane and use controlled firmware updates and configuration backups.

## RFQ Inputs

Send the installation country, certification scheme, hazardous-area classification, client list, coverage drawing, mounting height, mobile routes, traffic profile, backhaul, power, authentication, VLAN, controller, monitoring, document, inspection, and delivery requirements.
